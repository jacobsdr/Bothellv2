Your two-year arc

Use the pop-up as a learning lab.

🔎

Phase 1 · Months 1–6

Learn

Observe everything. What sells? Who stops? What questions repeat? Do not optimize yet; build a reliable baseline.

🧪

Phase 2 · Months 7–18

Adjust

Use what you learned. Change one thing at a time so you know what worked: layout, signage, pricing, product mix, or hours.

🧭

Phase 3 · Months 19–24

Decide

You have real data now. Make an informed decision about a storefront, markets, online growth, wholesale, or a graceful exit.

Setting up your space

An 8×10 space can feel like a cluttered closet or a focused boutique. The difference comes from a few deliberate choices.

The 3-second rule: Step outside your space and walk toward it like a stranger. If the first thing you see is not your strongest product or clearest sign, fix that before opening.
01

Create one hero product

Choose the product that best represents your brand and creates curiosity from outside the unit.

02

Vary height

Use risers, shelves, crates, or small stands so the display is not one flat table.

03

Make pricing visible

People often avoid asking. Clear prices reduce friction and make browsing more comfortable.

04

Group by story

Try themes such as “gift for her,” “weekend ritual,” or “everyday essential” instead of product categories only.

05

Leave breathing room

If every item touches another item, remove about one third of the display and test whether browsing improves.

06

Signal that you are open

Use your exterior space, chalkboard, open door, and visible presence to invite foot traffic.

Bridge online to in-person

You are not starting from zero. You already have followers, customers, and credibility online. Your task is to activate that audience and give them a reason to cross from screen to storefront.

Announcement post

“Exciting news — [Business Name] now has a physical home at Pop Shops on Main in downtown Bothell. If you have wanted to see the work in person, come say hi this weekend.”

Day-of reminder

“We are open today! Come see [specific product] in person. It is one of those pieces that is hard to fully appreciate in photos.”

Weekend recap

“Thank you for an incredible weekend. [Specific product] was the favorite, and I loved meeting online customers in person.”

How to actually sell

Think of your space less like a store and more like your home. Your job is making guests feel welcome, not forcing a transaction.

Say this

“Hey, welcome! Let me know if you have any questions.”

“That one is popular because [specific reason]. Want to take a closer look?”

“Of course. We are here through Sunday if you want to come back.”

Not this

“Can I help you find something?”

“That is one of my best sellers!” followed by silence.

“Are you sure? It is really popular.”

Buying signals: picking up the same item twice, asking about size/color/quantity, showing the item to a friend, or standing near one display for 30+ seconds.

Keep the rhythm simple and repeatable. Consistency matters more than polished content.

FAQ

What should I focus on in the first month?

Consistency and observation. Do not change everything immediately. Track what happens and look for patterns.

What if foot traffic is slow?

Use quiet time productively: improve signage, take content photos, talk with neighboring vendors, and update your tracker.

How often should I change my display?

Small refreshes monthly are enough. Bigger changes should be tested one at a time so you know what caused the result.
